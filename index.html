Loss magnitude

What each loss event costs. Primary loss always happens; secondary loss only when outside parties react.

Reference

Estimating good ranges

  • Start with a range so wide it is obviously true, then pull each end in until you would bet you are about 90% likely to be right.
  • Begin with what you do know: incident tickets, audit findings, insurance claims, published breach reports.
  • Break hard numbers into parts. Notification cost, for example, is records affected × cost per record.
  • Write the reasoning behind each range in the scope, so you can defend or revise it later.
  • Ask the people closest to the data: security operations, finance, legal.
  • A range that is right beats a single number that only looks precise.

Where the expected loss comes from

Model settings
Method

Counting events draws how many loss events happen in each simulated year, so quiet years show up as zero loss. The average method multiplies frequency by loss every year, the way simpler FAIR tools do. Both give the same expected loss; they differ in the tail.

The same seed replays the same random draws, so results only move when your inputs do. Try another seed to see how much comes from sampling noise alone.

Model notes

How the numbers are made

Each simulated year

  1. Every factor is drawn from its range using a PERT distribution, which peaks at your best guess and tapers towards the lowest and highest values.
  2. Loss event frequency is threat event frequency × vulnerability, or your direct estimate.
  3. The number of loss events that year is drawn from a Poisson distribution with that frequency as its average, so many years have none.
  4. Each event gets its own primary loss. With a probability equal to that year's secondary loss event frequency, it also triggers a secondary loss.
  5. The year's total is the sum. 10,000 years give the distribution behind every figure on the page.

Assumptions and limits

  • Factors are independent of one another, and loss events arrive independently. Clustered or linked attacks are not modelled.
  • In capability vs resistance mode, vulnerability is the share of 20,000 paired draws where capability beats resistance, then held fixed.
  • Years with more than 200 events use a normal approximation for their total loss.
  • For a rare scenario like the example, the expected loss moves by about ±3% between seeds. Read results to two significant figures.
  • Not included yet: comparing controls, losses broken out by the six forms, contact frequency and probability of action.
